Permits & utilities

Do I need a permit to demolish a house in Maryland?

In almost all cases, yes. Demolition (raze) permits for a building are issued by the county or, in some incorporated cities, by the city: for example Prince George's County DPIE or Montgomery County DPS. Smaller structures and interior work have their own rules. We confirm what applies to your address and file the permit as part of the job.

Who arranges the utility disconnects?

We do. Before a raze permit is issued, gas, electric, water and sewer generally have to be disconnected and capped by the utilities, such as WSSC Water, Pepco, BGE or SMECO depending on location. You may need to sign the request as the property owner, but we prepare the paperwork and follow up. Underground lines are also marked through Miss Utility before any digging.

Does my property need historic review?

If the property is a designated historic site or sits inside a historic district, demolition can require review by the local historic preservation commission. That review can add significant time, so we check for it before you commit to a schedule.

Hazardous materials

Do you remove asbestos and lead paint?

No. We do not perform asbestos or lead abatement ourselves. Where a survey is needed, we arrange it, and any abatement is carried out by licensed abatement contractors before our crew begins. We schedule that work so it fits the demolition program, and the survey and clearance paperwork goes in your file.

Does my building need an asbestos survey before demolition?

Commercial buildings, and residential buildings with more than four units, generally need an asbestos inspection before demolition under federal rules, with advance notice to the state agency (in Maryland, the Maryland Department of the Environment). Rules for single-family homes are different and vary locally, but older homes often contain asbestos, so a survey is usually wise. We will tell you what applies to your project.

Cost & timing

How much does demolition cost?

It depends on the size and construction of the structure, whether the foundation or basement comes out, hazardous materials, access for equipment, the volume of debris and how finished you want the site. Because two jobs of the same size can differ widely, we do not publish a flat rate. Photos and the address are enough for a budget range, and a site walk gives you a firm written price.

How long does a house demolition take?

The machine work for a typical single-family house is usually a matter of days, not weeks. The longer part is the preparation: permit review, utility disconnects and any abatement, which commonly take several weeks combined depending on the jurisdiction and utilities. We give you a realistic schedule with the quote and start the paperwork as soon as you approve it.

Do you remove the foundation and basement?

If you want it removed, yes. Most teardowns for a new build include removing the foundation, breaking out basement slabs and walls, and backfilling the hole with suitable fill. If your builder plans to reuse part of the foundation, we leave it and protect it. The quote states exactly what is removed and what stays.

What condition is the lot left in?

Debris is hauled to permitted facilities, voids are backfilled and the lot is rough graded, so it is ready for a surveyor and a builder. If you need erosion and sediment controls, stabilization or a specific finished grade, we include it in the scope. We walk the site with you at the end.

Working with us

What do you need from me to give a quote?

The property address, a few photos of the structure and the surrounding lot, what needs to come down and what must stay, and your timeline. Drawings, a survey or an asbestos report help if you have them. You can use the quote form, email {{EMAIL}} or call {{PHONE}}.

Can you demolish only part of a building or just the interior?

Yes. Full teardowns are the work we lead with, and we also remove part of a structure, such as an addition, porch or second story. Interior gut-outs, where the shell stays, are a separate service. Anything structural is removed under a plan from your engineer or architect, and the parts that stay are protected.

Do you recycle demolition debris?

Where it is practical. Metals are separated for recycling, and clean concrete is sent to a recycling facility where one will accept it. Disposal tickets for every load are kept and handed over at the end of the job, which is useful for lenders, buyers and sustainability reporting.

Do I need to be on site during demolition?

No. We walk the site with you before work starts and again at the end. During the work we keep you updated by phone or email, and for occupied or shared buildings we coordinate access with the building manager.

Permit, utility and environmental requirements change and vary by jurisdiction. These answers are general guidance; we confirm the specifics for your property before quoting.
