Demolition where the building stays standing

Interior demolition is different work from knocking a building down. The structure, the roof and often the neighbors stay in place, so the job is about control: knowing which walls are load-bearing, keeping dust out of the rest of the building, getting debris out through a hallway, an elevator or a single loading door, and leaving the space clean enough for the next trade to start the following morning.

This is one service, not the whole company. If the building itself needs to come down, see house demolition or commercial building demolition. Interior and selective demolition is for property owners, general contractors, landlords and tenants when the shell stays. We take the space back to whatever point your plans call for — a single room, a full floor, or a complete gut to the studs or the shell.

Interior demolition services

  • Full gut-outs — residential or commercial interiors stripped to studs, joists or the bare shell ahead of a renovation.
  • Tenant strip-outs — removing a previous tenant's fit-out at the end of a lease or before a new build-out.
  • Kitchen and bathroom tear-outs — cabinets, fixtures, tile, flooring and drywall removed and hauled.
  • Selective demolition — non-structural walls, ceilings, flooring, finishes and fixtures removed while adjacent areas stay in use.
  • Restaurant and retail removal — counters, millwork, ceilings and equipment taken out, with salvageable items set aside where that reduces your cost.
  • Debris removal — loading, hauling and disposal at permitted facilities, with tickets kept for your file.

Working inside

How we keep interior work under control

Make-safe first

Before anything is cut, electrical, plumbing and gas in the work area are shut off and made safe by the appropriate licensed trades. Walls are checked for live services before they come out.

Structure respected

Load-bearing elements are identified from drawings and on site. Anything structural is removed only under a plan from the project engineer or architect — never by guesswork.

Dust and noise managed

Work areas are separated with containment, floors and finishes outside the scope are protected, and noisy work is scheduled around building rules and neighbors.

Clean handover

Debris out, floors swept and the space walked with you or your superintendent so the next trade can start on time.

Occupied buildings

A lot of interior work happens in buildings where people are still living or working: an office floor above a business that stays open, a condo with neighbors on the other side of the wall, a retail unit in a busy center. On those jobs we agree the working hours, the debris route, the elevator or loading-dock schedule and the protection plan with the building owner or manager before we start, and we stick to it.

Hazardous materials in interiors

Older ceiling tiles, flooring and its adhesive, pipe insulation, drywall joint compound and paint can contain asbestos or lead. Disturbing them without a survey creates a risk to everyone in the building and can stop a job. We do not perform asbestos or lead abatement ourselves; where a survey is needed we arrange it, and licensed abatement contractors remove regulated materials before our crew begins.

Permits for interior demolition

Whether a permit is needed depends on the jurisdiction and the scope. Removing finishes may not need one; removing walls, or work that is part of a larger renovation, often does. In many cases interior demolition is covered by the general contractor's building permit. We confirm which applies with the local permitting office — for example Prince George's County DPIE, Montgomery County DPS or Anne Arundel County's permit office — before work begins.
